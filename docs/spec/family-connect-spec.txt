Family Connect: Product and Technical
Specification
 Oct 14, 2026  · @Rana  · Working name; v0.1 for the development team
1. Overview and goals
Family Connect is a voice-first AI companion that keeps older adults socially engaged at
home and strengthens, rather than replaces, their contact with adult children who live
away or are busy.
The problem. Loneliness affects roughly 1 in 5 older adults in India and about a third of
adults aged 50–80 in the US, and is linked to higher risk of dementia, heart disease, stroke
and depression. Many older people cannot or will not use smartphone apps, and their
children cannot be present every day.
The product. The parent talks by voice to an AI avatar on a tablet or TV, and to Alexa+ on
Echo devices throughout the home. The avatar is either a clearly labelled digital version of
their son or daughter (created and approved by the child) or a neutral companion with its
own name. It chats, remembers their life, plays real messages from family, reminds them
about medicines and tests, detects emergencies and escalates to real people. Alexa+
extends the experience to every room: help phrases, messages, reminders, everyday
tasks and smart-home safety. The adult child receives daily reports, conversation starters
and timely nudges to call or visit.
Goals for v1
Parent can start a conversation with zero touch, by saying a wake phrase.
Daily engaging conversation in Hindi, English or a mix.
Real calls and visits from family increase after onboarding (primary success metric).
Emergencies reach a real person within minutes, with layered backup.
Parent consent and privacy are enforced in code, not just policy.
Non-goals for v1
Avatars of deceased people (excluded by design).
A general-purpose companion for young people.
Medical advice, diagnosis or dose changes.
Building custom hardware.

2. Product principles
These are design constraints. Any feature that conflicts with them needs explicit sign-off.
1. Support connection, never replace it. The avatar fills gaps between real contact and
actively pushes toward calls and visits. Success is measured by real human contact,
not avatar usage time.
2. Always honest that it is AI. The child-avatar is labelled on screen at all times, refers to
itself as the "digital" version, and setup includes a family conversation explaining it.
3. Defer, don't decide. The avatar never makes commitments, decisions or promises on
behalf of the real child. Plans, money, health and family matters are passed to the real
person.
4. The child controls their avatar. Only the child can create their avatar, from their own
photo and voice, with explicit consent. They set boundaries and can read what "they"
said.
5. The parent controls their data. The parent agrees to what is shared in reports and can
keep things private.
6. Safety never depends on the AI alone. Emergency detection runs as a deterministic
engine alongside the LLM, with layered escalation to humans.
7. Remind, never advise on health. The system relays doctors' instructions and
reminders; it never interprets symptoms, changes doses or recommends treatment.
8. Alexa+ as a core channel, with no single point of failure. Alexa+ on Echo devices is a
standard part of every installation and the parent's voice access in every room. Safety-
critical paths are always duplicated through the companion app and our own
telephony, so no outage or platform change on one channel can stop an emergency
response.

5. System architecture
The product runs on two connected front ends in the home: the companion app (avatar,
camera, calls) and Alexa+ on Echo devices (whole-home voice, notifications, everyday
tasks, smart-home routines). Both talk to the same cloud backend and MCP tools, so
memory, boundaries and safety rules are identical on both. Safety-critical paths never
depend on a single channel.

5.2 Hardware for the pilot
Primary: Android tablet (LTE model preferred for backup connectivity) on a stand, plus
a USB or Bluetooth conference speakerphone for far-field hearing.
Standard: Echo Dot with Alexa+ in the bedroom and near the bathroom; optional Echo
Show in the kitchen or second living area. Linked to the parent's Amazon account
during setup.
Later: big-screen option on Android TV or Fire TV Cube with USB webcam. Avoid Vega
OS Fire TV Sticks (4K Select and later), which cannot run Android apps.
Audio rule: each conversation uses one device for both speaking and listening. At the
tablet, the avatar's video and voice both come from the tablet with a conference
speakerphone (loud speaker plus echo-cancelling microphones). In other rooms, the
parent talks to Priya by voice on the Echo, and Alexa can offer to move the conversation
to the tablet. Do not split video on the tablet and voice on an Echo: Bluetooth delay breaks
lip-sync, a microphone on one device cannot cancel a speaker on another, and Alexa skills
cannot stream real-time conversation in sync with another device.
6. Key flows
6.1 Starting a conversation
1. Parent says "Hello Priya" to the tablet (on-device wake phrase), or "Alexa, talk to Priya"
to an Echo.
2. For Echo: Alexa skill request → session gateway → push to tablet app.
3. App wakes screen, shows the labelled avatar (or neutral companion), opens a
streaming voice session.
4. Orchestrator loads persona, boundaries, memory and today's context (messages
waiting, reminders due, recent mood).
5. Conversation runs; safety signals stream to the safety engine in parallel.
6. Session ends on silence or "bye"; orchestrator writes memory updates and report
notes.

6.2 Deferral ("Will you come for Diwali?")
1. Guardrail classifier tags the turn as a commitment request.
2. Avatar replies with a deferral ("Let me check with the real me and get back to you").
3. create_deferral  tool posts the item to the child's app with context.
4. Child answers in the app (text, voice note or call).
5. Avatar relays the real answer next session, attributed to the real child.
6.3 Emergency escalation
1. Trigger: help phrase (tablet or Echo), distress detected, fall detected, or no response to
a check-in after a set window.
2. Safety engine opens an incident and checks with the parent by voice ("Are you
okay?").
3. Not an emergency (parent responds and declines help, or concern is low): follow-up
check and Important alert to the child by call and WhatsApp (F-32).
4. Emergency (parent asks for an ambulance or help; or help request or fall followed by
no response): spoken countdown, "Calling emergency services in 10 seconds. Say
'cancel' if you're okay."
5. At zero, in parallel: dial 112 or 108 (cellular route on LTE tablet preferred, SIP if verified);
SIP call to primary child with IVR; SIP call to backup contact; dispatch to response
partner where configured.
6. Emergency call is bridged into the tablet speaker and microphone so the operator talks
to the parent; recorded opener states the address; child is conferenced in when they
answer.
7. With prior consent, camera view becomes available to the child or responder; indicator
on screen and spoken notice.
8. Address, medical summary, medicines and trigger are sent to child, backup and
response partner by WhatsApp and in-app.
9. Every step is logged; incident closes only when a human confirms the parent is safe.
6.4 Medicine reorder
1. Parent: "Hello Priya, my BP tablets are running out."
2. Orchestrator calls check_prescription  and get_refill_status .
3. draft_order  creates an order for exactly the prescribed item and quantity with the
partner pharmacy.
4. Child receives an approval request; nothing is paid until approved.

5. On approval, order is placed; avatar confirms to the parent; Echo notification on

6.5 Prescription or report scan
1. Child photographs the document in the family app.
2. Extraction service returns medicines, doses, timings and test dates with confidence
scores.
3. Child reviews and edits every field; low-confidence fields are highlighted.
4. On confirmation, reminders are scheduled and the prescription is stored for reorders.
6.6 Daily report and nudge
1. Throughout the day, the orchestrator tags wellbeing signals from conversations (mood,
energy, sleep, appetite, pain or illness mentions, worries) and adherence events.
2. Alert engine scores each signal against the three tiers (F-71); Urgent and Important
alerts are sent immediately as text and voice note via WhatsApp, push and in-app.
3. At the child's chosen time, the digest job summarises the period: mood, activity, topics,
deferrals, adherence, and 2–3 conversation starters from the parent's own stories.
4. Text-to-speech generates a 30–60 second voice note of the digest.
5. Delivery through a pre-approved WhatsApp template with voice note, quick-reply
buttons and a link to the full report in the app.
6. Call nudge: checks call cadence against the child's interval and today's mood; adds a
nudge if due or if the parent seemed low.
7. Parent privacy flags are applied before sending; safety items are always included;
sensitive health detail stays behind the app link.
7. MCP tool design and data model
All AI actions go through MCP tool servers. The same tools serve the companion app, the
Alexa skill/agent and any future channel, and the LLM provider can be swapped without
changing them. Permission, consent and boundary checks are enforced inside each
server, never left to the model.

Integration details
Account linking: OAuth from the skill to Family Connect, completed by the child in the
family app during setup.
Same brain on both channels: Alexa requests call the same orchestrator and MCP
tools as the app, so memory, persona, boundaries and defer-don't-decide behave
identically.
Hand-off to screen: when the parent wants to see the avatar or a video call, Alexa says
"I've opened Priya on your tablet" and the gateway pushes the session to the app.
Echo Show: used for Alexa visuals (message cards, reminders) where helpful; the live
avatar stays on the companion app.
Known constraints to design around: Echo wake words are a fixed list (no custom names,
so the Echo phrase is "Alexa, talk to Priya"); third-party skills cannot render a custom live
avatar on Echo Show or access its camera; Echo cannot call regular phone numbers or
emergency numbers in India (Alexa-to-Alexa calling only). Amazon discontinued Alexa
Together in May 2025, so every safety path through Alexa is duplicated through the
companion app and our SIP telephony.

9. Safety, privacy and compliance
9.1 AI safety guardrails
System design, not just prompts: a guardrail classifier checks each user turn and each
avatar response for commitments, health advice, disallowed topics and distress.
Hard blocks: no medical advice or dose changes; no financial actions; no impersonation
outside the labelled avatar; no claims about the real child's life that are not in
confirmed data.
Anti-dependency: avatar regularly bridges outward (family, local groups); reports flag
rising avatar time alongside falling real contact.
Evaluation set: scripted conversations covering deferrals, boundary breaches,
confusion, distress and medical questions, run before every model or prompt change.
9.2 Privacy
On-device wake phrase; audio leaves the device only after the wake phrase or an
active session.
Camera: fall detection runs on device; no video leaves the home except a live view
during an active incident, with prior consent and a visible and spoken indicator. No
recording by default.
Parent privacy flags respected in all reports; safety-related information is always
shared.
